## WHAT I SAW ON OUR OWN PAGES (renders r6, 1440 and 390)
The headlines do not read massive because they are large. They read massive because the SAME gesture repeats five times down the page at the same scale: the tagline, the three words crossing the pictures, the six service words, GROWING? GOOD., the inner-page title. One face, one weight, one case, hard left, every time. There is no middle size anywhere between 3.5vw body heads and 10 to 17vw display, so nothing gives the giant a scale to be giant against. On the phone the 17vw stack is four lines of stencil with a word per line, which is a poster with no picture.

## REFERENCES LOOKED AT THIS TURN (headless Chrome, 1440 wide, full page)

1. Signal-A Studio, https://signal-a.studio/ (Signal-A, Warsaw and Berlin; Awwwards Site of the Day 18 Oct 2026).
DID: one paragraph set as the whole first screen, force-justified so every line runs full measure, in a light condensed serif with three words switched to a heavy grotesk (AWARD-WINNING, DESIGN, STUDIO). Word spacing opens wildly and they let it. A tiny bracketed label sits centred above; a scattered six-word strap sits far below in 10px caps.
TAKE: scale contrast made inside one block by WEIGHT and FAMILY, not by size. The heavy words carry the sentence; the light words are the connective. Our two faces do exactly this: General Sans 400 for the connective, Bespoke Stencil 700 for the three load-bearing words, same line.
REJECT: the mixed serif and grotesk; we have no serif and must not add one. The strap scattered across the width is a 2024 tic.
Felt: my eye went straight to the three black words and read the sentence backwards from them. Second look held.

2. Exo Ape, https://www.exoape.com/ (Exo Ape, Netherlands).
DID: three words, one per line, in a grotesk at about 14vw, but the block sits BELOW a small five-line intro paragraph and ABOVE a second paragraph, so the giant is bracketed by text at reading size. Huge top margin; the word block starts a third of the way down.
TAKE: the bracket. A giant line earns its size when small text sits directly above and below it at a fixed distance. Our hero has the who-block far away and nothing below; put 13px text 24px under the last stencil line and the stencil stops shouting.
REJECT: Digital Design Experience is the median studio sentence; and the three-stack itself is now everywhere (see dated moves).
Felt: calm, and I forgot it by the second scroll. Noted as mechanic, not as a piece.

3. XK Studio, https://xk.studio/ (XK, featured hoverstat.es 20 Jul 2026).
DID: the entire first screen is near-black with three 9px nav words top left; the work arrives as a strip of thumbnails pinned to the bottom edge, each with a 9px label. No headline at all. Type is the smallest thing on the page; the picture strip is the composition.
TAKE: the inversion. One section of our site can go type-small, picture-large: the work page or the three-picture row, with the stencil word at 2vw as a label, not a banner. Contrast across sections is what makes the stencil hero land when it does come.
REJECT: the whole-site version; a studio that sells words cannot have none.
Felt: relief after our pages. That is data: the eye wanted a rest it never got.

4. Brandon Yasin, https://brandonyasin.com/ (Brandon Yasin, portfolio; Awwwards Site of the Day 24 Oct 2026).
DID: warm off-white ground, a hand-drawn monogram centred at 40px, four-column 9px caps at the corners (name, menu, local time), then two thirds of a screen of nothing, then a row of three photographs with 9px titles above. The first screen is empty on purpose; the pictures are the first large thing.
TAKE: corners-and-void. Small type at the four corners of a fixed frame makes any void read as intent instead of a gap (our 390 render has the 425px void Anwar flagged; this is how it becomes a frame).
REJECT: the off-white ground (our avoid list), the monogram-as-logo, and the empty hero on a studio site that must say what it is.
Felt: nothing on the first screen, then the castle photograph with the projected 50 held me. The type never did.

5. Basement Studio, https://basement.studio/ (Basement, Argentina and US).
DID: a plain three-line grotesk headline at about 4.5vw, sentence case, then a logo wall of 36 clients in a six-column grid of identical boxes, then Featured Projects in a two-column row with a tiny spec list.
TAKE: the headline size. 4.5vw with sentence case reads as a studio speaking, not a poster. A size for our inner-page titles sits here, not at 11vw.
REJECT: the logo grid is the box failure in its purest form; the hero line reads as a pitch deck.
Felt: bored by the second screen. Bland is the verdict.

6. The Row, https://www.therow.com/ (The Row, New York).
DID: one black and white photograph, centred, two thirds of the width, no headline; the wordmark 12px, the nav 10px; then white space, then footer. The whole fashion-house page is one picture and two rows of small caps.
TAKE: the ratio. When the picture is the sentence, every piece of type is one size and small. For our film-led sections (the lane film, the Chhaya billboard) the stencil word on top of the picture at 10vw competes; at 2.5vw it captions and the picture gets to be expensive.
REJECT: the whole: we are not a house with a famous name; silence is a privilege they bought.
Felt: the quiet is the point and I respected it more than I enjoyed it.

## THREE COMPOSITION IDEAS FOR THIS SITE (each a different structure, not a size)
1. THE WEIGHTED SENTENCE (from Signal-A). One sentence, full measure, General Sans 400 at 3.2vw for the connective words, Bespoke Stencil 700 at the same cap height for the three words that carry it; the orange on one of the three. The giant disappears; the contrast does the work. On the phone it still wraps as one paragraph instead of a four-line poster.
2. THE BRACKETED GIANT (from Exo Ape and The Row). Keep ONE stencil line per page at 12vw and only one; 13px text sits 24px above and 24px below it at fixed distance, and every other heading on that page drops to 2.5vw stencil as a caption on a picture. Scale is restored by giving the giant something small to stand against.
3. THE PLATE AND THE VOID (from Brandon Yasin and XK, plus our own mark3d). First screen: four 9px corner labels frame the viewport; the steel stencil plate with the flame cut through it sits off-centre as the only large object; the stencil tagline is NOT on the first screen at all. It arrives on the first scroll, rising behind the plate at 8vw, lit by the flame, and parks under it. The 3D carries the type into view instead of sitting beside it. Posh is the plate rendered as brushed steel with one light, not a glow.
