### 1. Clauses 26 and 30, AI and recording: VETO (can be cleared)

The current words:
- **Clause 26:** "No recording or sharing happens unless both sides agree first. This includes AI note-taking tools and meeting bots that join the call. We take our own written notes."
- **Clause 30:** "The people who see it are our own team. We pass it to nobody else."
- **Clause 24** also bans the guest from using the read-out to "train software".

The evidence:
- **The studio's own rules** (`rules/narrative-chain-default.md` and `rules/no-ai-watermark.md`) cover any written piece that leaves this Mac. Claude seats plan it and judge it, and the local model types it. So what the guest shares reaches Anthropic.
- **Anthropic's consumer Max plans** keep chats for 30 days when the training setting is off. When it is on, they keep chats for 5 years and may train on them.
- **The studio's Google AI Ultra account** includes Google Meet's "Take notes for me". It is one click away on the call.
- **The Gemini twin** would make it worse if a brief ever carried the material there. Google keeps chats that its staff have reviewed for up to 3 years, even after the user deletes them.

The Bok test fails. A guest who has just read "no AI note-takers, only our team sees it" would feel differently if they knew how the read-out is made. The page bans the guest's AI tools and says nothing about the studio's own.

How to clear it. The work picks one of two routes:
- **Route A, say it plainly.**
  - State that the studio uses AI tools to help prepare the read-out, and that a person at the studio decides and checks everything in it.
  - Name those services in the privacy notice.
  - Promise "not used to train AI" only after someone has checked that the training setting is off on all three Claude accounts.
  - Keep audit material under `~/FlowState-Clients/`, so the twin guard keeps it away from Gemini.
  - State that the studio never turns on Meet's note-taker or any recorder without first asking in writing, and that saying no costs the guest nothing.
- **Route B, change how the work is done.** Audit material never reaches any AI service outside this Mac. Only then can clause 30 stand as written.

Asif must also rule on one clash. The no-watermark rule ("never hint that a machine wrote it") cannot be read as permission to leave a data-handling service out of the privacy terms. The awards exception he already made is the model for that ruling.

### 2. Clause 32, the 12-month delete: FIX

Current words: "We keep audit material for 12 months, then delete it, unless you become a paid client."

The problem: audit material can end up in many places. These include Gmail and Google Drive, phones and WhatsApp, local Claude chat records, the studio's memory index, git history, backups, the Discord work feed, and the AI services' own servers.

What must change:
- **Define "audit material":** the form answers, any files the guest sends, the studio's call notes and its copy of the read-out.
- **Give each audit one home:** one folder under `~/FlowState-Clients/` and one email label. Nothing goes into the work feed, the memory index or git.
- **Promise only what can be shown:** deletion from those named places at 12 months, then an email to the guest saying it is done.
- **Name the honest exceptions:**
  - Backups clear on their real cycle. State that cycle only once it has been measured.
  - The services named in the privacy notice delete on their own schedules.
- **Add the right to ask for deletion sooner**, confirmed by email.
- **Say what happens** to the material of people still on the waiting list when the offer ends.
- **Set up the dated deletion task** before the first audit is taken.

### 3. The waiting list: FIX

The current words:
- **Short version:** "If you are on the waiting list, that position promises nothing."
- **Clause 10:** "The offer is for a limited time, with no fixed end date."
- **Clause 34:** "We use this power for these five reasons only, never for any other."
- **Clause 36:** "We may change or end the offer."

The problems:
- **The star lands on a short version that misses the limit.** The star beside "Free for a limited time" opens a short version that never says there are 10 places, or that the offer can end early. The real limit is a number of places, not a date.
- **Clause 35 adds a sixth reason.** It lets the studio hold an audit for a conflict of interest, which only the studio judges. That contradicts clause 34's "five reasons only".
- **Clause 36 leaves a door open.** As written, a change to the offer could add a way of picking applicants.
- **The definition overpromises.** "Holding a place means you will get the call and the read-out" is contradicted by clauses 18, 19, 34 and 35.

What must change:
- **The short version opens with the limit:** 10 places, given in order, ending after the tenth call or earlier if the studio ends it, with no set date.
- **Say what a waiting-list position does promise** (your turn, in order) and what it does not (a place).
- **Clause 36 states** that a change never reorders the list or adds any way of choosing between applicants.
- **Clause 34 names clause 35** as the only other case where an audit waits, and says it is not a broken rule.
- **Clause 35 says** what happens to a held place while a conflict lasts, and what happens if the offer ends during it.
- **The definition of "a place"** points to the clauses that can end one.

### 4. Fairness: FIX

The current words:
- **Clause 12** lets the studio off for "something outside our control".
- **Clause 21** lets the studio move a call as often as it needs to.
- **The guest gets:** "A second move means the place goes to the next person on the waiting list" (clause 18) and "we wait 15 minutes, then the call is closed" (clause 19).

In Dhaka, power cuts, internet outages (such as the 2024 shutdown), strikes and floods are ordinary. A power cut ten minutes before the call costs the guest their place.

What must change:
- **The guest gets the same grace as the studio.** If something outside the guest's control happens and they email as soon as they can, they get one new time. That new time does not count as their one move.
- **A late start by the studio is not the guest's loss.** If the studio joins late, the guest gets the full 60 minutes or a new time.
- **Clause 34(5)** currently reads "Rude, threatening or abusive behaviour":
  - Drop "rude". It is a low bar that only the studio judges, and a direct style in a second language can read as rude when it isn't.
  - Keep threatening, abusive and harassing.
  - Give one written warning first, except for threats.
- **Clause 5** currently reads "a business that sells branding, design, or business consulting":
  - As written it catches print shops and sign-makers.
  - Limit it to businesses whose main trade is selling those services to other companies.
  - Invite anyone unsure to ask before applying.
  - An honest applicant who turns out to fall inside it leaves the list without being treated as having given false details.
- **Clause 34(3):** a meeting bot that joins the call on its own only counts as a broken rule if the guest refuses to remove it.

### 5. What is missing: FIX

What must be added:
- **Who and which language:** who from the studio is on the call, by role, and whether the call and read-out are in English or Bangla. Also whether the guest may bring a colleague or an adviser.
- **How to withdraw** or leave the waiting list: one email to the contact address.
- **How to ask for deletion.**
- **The privacy notice** that clauses 30 and 32 point to:
  - I did not find it at `privacy.html` or `privacy/index.html` in this worktree. I tried only those two paths, not a full search.
  - It must exist before this page ships, and it must name every service that stores or handles audit material, AI services included.
  - Bangladesh's Personal Data Protection Ordinance 2025 was published in the official gazette on 6 November 2025, so this notice is a legal requirement, not a courtesy.
