B. RE-WORD (quoted exactly; the fault; what the fix must achieve)

1. The short version, the six sentences left at line 5 from "You get one video call of up to 60 minutes." to "You owe us nothing for this offer." Fault: six sentences of nearly one length, a metronome; "strictly" is an intensifier; "that position promises nothing" makes a queue position act. Must achieve: four or five sentences carrying the same five facts (one call, one read-out in 10 working days, order of arrival, one per company, nothing owed), one of them noticeably short, said the way a person explains a rule to a friend; the waiting-list sentence describes a place in a line, not a promise, with no actor behind it.

2. Clause 6: "There are 10 places. They go to complete applications by the date and time we receive them. First come, first served. Nothing else decides the order." Fault: two fragments stacked; staccato against the measured register. Must achieve: one or two complete sentences, keeping first come first served as the child's version of the rule, and keeping the promise that nothing else decides the order.

3. Clause 7: "Within 2 working days we send you the confirmation email." Fault: repeats the heading word for word. Must achieve: heading as a short label, body carrying the two working days once, no shared phrasing.

4. Clause 3: "A company that has already applied cannot try again under a new name or through another person." Fault: as written it bars a company that only withdrew. Must achieve: the bar applies only to a company that has had its audit or lost its place, and names the spec's three disguises: a different name, a new trading name, another person.

5. Clause 5: "People from competing studios cannot take part" and "If this comes to light later, clause 34 (Five rules, and what happens when one is broken) applies." Fault: cold; "comes to light" is the detective's phrase. Must achieve: a heading that says who the offer is not for in a host's tone; a pointer sentence that says briefly what happens if we learn this afterwards, and ends at the pointer.

6. Clause 10 tail: "When the offer ends, the waiting list closes. Everyone still on it is told by email within 5 working days. Their position ends. It does not carry over to any later offer unless we say so in writing." Fault: four clipped sentences for one idea. Must achieve: two complete sentences, every fact kept (list closes, email within 5 working days, position ends, no carry-over unless we say so in writing).

7. Clause 13: "We do not carry out any of the work the read-out suggests." and "There are no extra calls, unlimited follow-up questions, or rewriting of the read-out." Fault: the first repeats the heading; the second repeats clause 14's rule on rewriting. Must achieve: the body lists the concrete things this is not (design, writing, building, strategy, extra calls, unlimited questions) without re-saying the heading or mentioning rewriting.

8. Clause 16 heading: "The read-out rests on what you share". Fault: a document made to act. Must achieve: a heading that says, as a plain request from us to you, that we need honest and complete information.

9. Clause 17: "False details break these terms" and "If we find false details, clause 34 (Five rules, and what happens when one is broken) applies: we may take the application off the waiting list, or pause or end the audit, and we tell you why." Fault: the heading accuses; the sentence repeats clause 34's consequences, which the spec reserves to 34. Must achieve: a heading that asks for true details rather than threatens; "This includes a false company name" must still make sense under it; the pointer sentence ends at the pointer. "An honest mistake that you correct when we ask is not a false detail." stays as written; it is the warmest line on the page.

10. Clause 27: "This audit is an honest view of your business, not professional advice." Fault: "honest" is the studio praising itself; the body leaves the four kinds of advice to the heading. Must achieve: the body says plainly what this is not (legal, financial, tax, accounting advice) and sends the reader to a qualified person, with no self-description.

11. Clause 29: "You owe us nothing for the audit and are under no obligation to buy anything." Fault: the heading and the short version both just said it. Must achieve: the body opens on the other half (no obligation to buy, none on us to offer paid work) without the heading's words.

12. Clause 34: "We use this power for these five reasons only, never for any other." and "A pause is a hold used while we check something. During a pause you keep your place, and we tell you when we will decide." Fault: "power" is guard vocabulary; "a hold" defines a pause with its synonym; "when we will decide" reads as the studio choosing at leisure. Must achieve: the five reasons stated as a limit we put on ourselves; a pause explained as a short wait while we check whether a rule was really broken, during which you keep your place and we give you a date by which you hear the result.
