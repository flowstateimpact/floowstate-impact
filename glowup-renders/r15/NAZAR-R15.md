# NAZAR R15: how expensive studio sites move the reader

Nazar, the taste scout, 31 October 2026. For Nadir (builds the bar and the in-page navigator) and Anwar (judges). R14 stands; this is only about movement. All read live by address this run. LOOKED AT means a headless Chrome shot at 1440 x 900, shrunk to 800 px; READ means the page's own HTML and class names, where the mechanics live. Vallee Duhamel would not resolve; Hello Monday rendered black and is not counted. Shots are in this run's scratchpad shots folder.

## The references, what each maker did

1. Pentagram, https://www.pentagram.com/work and https://www.pentagram.com/work/520-fifth-avenue (maker: Pentagram). LOOKED AT and READ. Top bar: serif wordmark left, five words right, white ground, a sticky header that tracks scroll. The work index has NO filter widget in the page. The filter is a sentence fixed to the bottom of the viewport, 24 px up, "We design [Everything] for [Everyone]", each bracket a dropdown of disciplines or sectors; The case page ends with a full-width hairline, the two words "Next Project" in the secondary grey, then the next case's own title block, which is sticky at the bottom (data-stickybottom-header) so it rides up as you finish. Page changes swap inside one container (pjax) so the bar never reloads. TAKE: the switcher as a sentence pinned to the viewport bottom, and the case hand-over as a rule plus two words plus the next title. Felt: the only filter I have ever wanted to click.

2. Koto, https://koto.studio/work and https://koto.studio/work/projects (maker: Koto, London). LOOKED AT and READ. The bar is a dark left column: wordmark, a "WORK" crumb fixed top left in exclusion blend so it inverts over whatever scrolls under it, then the two kinds (Projects, Partnerships) as an 11 px mono list. The active kind is white with a 3 px dot beside it; the inactive is mid grey, its dot scaled to zero and growing on hover; the one-line description under each kind is a collapsed box that opens on the active one. The hovered case appears bottom left as a small card: logo, title, sector word. A single FILTER button sits fixed bottom right; its options are problem types carried in the address (?filter=digital-experiences, motion-systems, campaign). TAKE: a short text list with one dot as the only state, the kind written into the URL. REFUSE: the whole sidebar; we lack the archive to fill a third column. Felt: envy at the dot doing all the work.

3. UNIT9, https://www.unit9.com and https://www.unit9.com/work (maker: UNIT9, London; films, interactive, experiential). LOOKED AT (the archive) and READ (the live nav). The kinds of work ARE the top bar: SEARCH, EXP, INTERACTIVE, FILM, ABOUT, CONTACT, all caps, no "Work" word at all. Their old archive (archive.unit9.com) reads basic: a black strip of six dropdowns, a "Showing results for" line, a uniform three-up grid with titles centred on the images. TAKE: kind-of-work words in the bar itself. REFUSE: the dropdown strip and centred-title grid, the shop filter.

4. Stink Studios, https://www.stinkstudios.com/work and https://www.stinkstudios.com/work/loewe-180-anniversary (maker: Stink Studios; film, digital, campaigns). READ. The header carries four states as class names: isAtTheTop, isScrollingUp, isDark, isPageTransitioning, and its background is a CSS variable (--background-opacity: 0) that the states turn up. So the bar is transparent at the top, leaves on scroll down, returns on the first scroll up, and holds still while the page changes. The hand-over sits at the TOP of the case page, inside the hero title block in the caption size: "back to all / next project", staggered in with the title. TAKE: the four-state bar, and the next link in the case title, not only at the foot. Felt: the cleverest thing here is invisible, which is the point.

5. ManvsMachine, https://mvsm.com (maker: ManvsMachine, London and Los Angeles; film and design). LOOKED AT and READ. Bar: the name and a one-line descriptor left, two words right (Work, Info), one icon; a custom cursor, and a header class named symmetrical-transitions, so the page leaves the way it arrived. Index: tiles of two widths, the title set INSIDE the image top left as two lines, "Gilga Farm / for Moncler", white, no caption row. TAKE: two words in the bar, the title inside the picture. REFUSE: the tile grid, still equal corners. Felt: "X for Y" titles beat any caption strip.

6. Nice and Serious, https://niceandserious.com/work and /work/branding (maker: Nice and Serious, London; branding, campaigns, film, websites). READ. The kind switcher is a row of plain words under the page title (All work, Branding, Campaigns, Film), each a REAL address, each word printed twice in the markup, once regular and once heavy, so the active word can change weight without the row shifting width. Page changes run through barba.js, and the switcher is marked barba-prevent so changing kind does not fire the page transition. TAKE: the doubled word (weight change without reflow), kinds as addresses. REFUSE: nothing.

7. Locomotive, https://locomotive.ca/en/work (maker: Locomotive, Montreal). READ. Every nav word carries data-hover-shuffle (the letters shuffle on hover), the active link is a -current class, the page scrolls on their own smooth-scroll module (data-scroll, has-scroll-smooth). Thin read. TAKE: one hover behaviour shared by every link in the bar. REFUSE: the letter shuffle, Dated.

## What reads basic by contrast, seen this run
A strip of dropdowns above a uniform grid (UNIT9's archive). Titles centred on every tile. A "Showing results for" line. Filled pill tabs and a desktop hamburger: none of the seven use either.

## The belt, mechanics only, by address
- Bar that leaves on scroll down and returns on scroll up: Aceternity "Floating Navbar", https://ui.aceternity.com/components/floating-navbar (a sticky bar that hides on scroll down, returns on scroll up). Take the scroll-direction logic only.
- Sticky in-page kind switcher with one moving indicator: Motion Primitives "Animated Background", https://motion-primitives.com/docs/animated-background (slides one background under the hovered or active item; use it for Koto's dot or a hairline, not a filled pill). shadcn "Tabs", https://ui.shadcn.com/docs/components/tabs, for keyboard and focus (arrow keys, aria-selected), styled to nothing.
- Knowing which kind is on screen as the reader scrolls: Motion Primitives "In View", https://motion-primitives.com/docs/in-view (IntersectionObserver wrapper).
- Not for us: Magic UI "Dock", https://magicui.design/docs/components/dock (Trying Too Hard); 21st.dev tabs, https://21st.dev/s/tabs, 239 pill variants, the median itself.

## The three moves, ranked
1. The kinds live in the bar, and the bar reads scroll direction (UNIT9 live nav, Stink's four states). Websites, Films, Pictures stand in the top bar as words; the bar is transparent at the top, gone on scroll down, back on the first scroll up, still during page change.
2. The in-page navigator is a short text list with one state mark, pinned to the viewport, each kind an address (Koto's dot and URL, Nice and Serious's doubled word, Pentagram's bottom-pinned sentence). Orange goes only on the state mark.
3. The hand-over is a hairline, two words and the next title, at the foot of every work page, and a small "back / next" in the case title at the top (Pentagram foot, Stink head).

Felt line: the sites that moved me had the fewest moving parts; each spends its one effect on the state mark and lets the words sit still.
