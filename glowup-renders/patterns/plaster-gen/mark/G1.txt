Keep the plaster, the fine comb grooves, the warm near-black colour and the dim even light of the first reference picture exactly. Take the shape only from the second reference picture, the studio's mark: a ribbon of flame lying on its side that swells and tapers in one long S from left to right, with thin tapered slits cut inside it. Straight-on photograph of a real hand-finished wall panel of lime plaster tinted the colour of wet charcoal with a faint brown undertone. Across the panel, one hand has combed that ribbon into the wet plaster at giant scale: two or three broad bands, each one a single unbroken pull with a fine notched comb, following the mark's swell and taper, so the grooves run along the ribbon's length and bend with it. The thin slits of the mark are long tapered channels where the comb was lifted and the plaster left smooth and slightly deeper. Torn lips of pushed-up plaster sit where each pull starts and ends. The bands are so large that the frame crops them on the top, right and bottom edges, the way the mark is cropped on the front of a printed card; around them is flat troweled plaster with fine sand tooth and pinholes. A long band of low light from far off to the left grazes the surface; the plaster stays close to black and only ridge tops catch a dull warm grey; the light is equally dim at the left edge, the centre and the right edge.
