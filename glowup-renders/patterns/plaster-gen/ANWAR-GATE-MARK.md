- The mark has three features that identify it: a hump on the left, a long inner slit that ends in a hook, and a tail that rises and narrows to a point on the right.
- G2 has all three, in the mark's own places. The hump crest is near source x 700, y 100. The slit runs from about (800, 330) to a hook at about (1650, 1020). The tail narrows to a point with a torn knot near (2500, 870).
- G2 also has one extra arc that is not in the mark. It enters at the top near x 1990 and leaves the right edge near y 700.
- G1 has the hump and a slit. Its three bands are parallel and the same width, and they leave the right edge at full width. There is no narrowing tail.

- After the bake, the grooves, the torn ridges and both slits in G2 are all still visible. Baked G2: middle value 15.9, top 1 percent at 35.3.
- In every desktop window, the second headline line ("WILL NOT DARE") runs along the nearly flat tail band. The tail's point lands inside DARE's box.

## CONCLUDED (my eye, not a tested panel)

1. The stranger test. Yes, it is plaster. Yes for G2, someone who has seen the logo would say "that's their mark". What gives it away is the tail narrowing to a point, which nothing else on a wall does. G1 fails this: three equal bands sweeping off the edge is the stock "flow" wave any wellness brand owns. That makes it DERIVATIVE.
2. The pick is G2. Its tail is the signature, and it puts the flame's tip under DARE. So the one orange word lands exactly where the flame burns. That rhyme is the emotional move the page lacked.
3. The bake does not kill the relief. The ribbon is drawn by the difference between combed and smooth texture. That detail is far smaller than the 220 px blur, so the flatten removes only the light falloff. Keep the recipe unchanged. No regrade is needed: the per-channel black step already removes G2's 6 percent lift, and the files were never browner.
4. The extra arc stays. It reads as a second ribbon too big for the wall, not as a repeat, so it is not logo wallpaper.
