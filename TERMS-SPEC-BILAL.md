## 1. Page purpose and reader

PURPOSE: The star on every audit button points here. This page says plainly what the free audit is, what it is not, and what each side can expect, so that nobody is surprised later.

READER: A small business owner who has never met us, reading on a phone, maybe in a second language. A six-year-old should be able to say back what the deal is ("you get one talk and one written letter about your business, it is free, and you wait your turn"). The tone is calm and respectful: a host explaining the house rules, never a guard reading out penalties. No legal words used for show. Where a legal idea is needed, it is said in everyday words first.

WRITING NOTES FOR KOLOM AND EHSAN:
- Open with the short version (one free call, one written read-out, waiting-list order, one per company), then the full terms.
- Each clause gets a plain heading that states its point ("You keep your read-out", not "Intellectual Property").
- Nothing may say or hint that the studio judges who gets a place: no shortlisting, no curating, no "successful applicants". Places go in order. Full stop.
- No em dashes. No word from the studio ban list (skills/knowledge/ban-lists.md).
- Never mention Akij in any form on this page.
- The page never sells the studio. It explains the offer and stops.

## 2. The clauses

### Words used on this page (sits directly under the short version, before clause 1; unnumbered so clause numbers do not move)

- "WE", "US" AND "THE STUDIO" mean FlowState Impact, registered in Dhaka, Bangladesh. The page states no company type and uses no other company name (Asif's ruling).
- "A PLACE" means one of the 10 free audits. Holding a place means you will get the call and the read-out.
- "THE WAITING LIST" is the line of people who have applied but do not yet hold a place. Being on it is not a place.
- "THE READ-OUT" is the written letter about your business that we send after the call.
- "THE CONFIRMATION EMAIL" is the email of clause 7. It is the only document besides these terms that forms part of the agreement (clause 37).
  WHY: The draft never said who "we" is, and "place" and "list" were used for the same thing, which is how clauses 9 and 10 came to contradict each other. Five words defined once remove the confusion everywhere.

### A. Who can take part

1. ONE AUDIT PER COMPANY. Each company can have one free audit, once.
   WHY: Stops one business taking repeat free consulting; keeps the 10 places fair for others waiting.

2. RELATED COMPANIES COUNT AS ONE. Companies under the same owner, the same group, or the same management count as one company for this offer.
   WHY: Closes the loophole of a group booking an audit for each of its brands, which would turn a free taster into a free group review.

3. NO RE-APPLYING UNDER ANOTHER NAME. A company that has had its audit, or lost its place under these terms, cannot apply again under a different name, a new trading name or through another person.
   WHY: Protects the waiting list from people jumping back in line; protects us from repeat free work.

4. THE PERSON APPLYING MUST BE ABLE TO SPEAK FOR THE BUSINESS. The applicant must be the owner, a director, or someone the owner has allowed to apply and share business details.
   WHY: Protects the client from an employee sharing private business facts without permission; protects us from acting on details nobody was allowed to give.

5. PEOPLE WHO RUN OR WORK FOR A COMPETING STUDIO OR CONSULTANCY CANNOT TAKE PART. This includes applying on their behalf or for their clients. A competing studio means a business that sells branding, design or business consulting to other companies. If this comes to light later, clause 34 applies: we may end the audit and we tell you why.
   WHY: The audit shows our working method; it is offered to businesses to help them, not to rivals studying how we work. Written as an eligibility rule, the same for everyone. The consequence now lives in one place (clause 34) so this clause and clause 34 cannot disagree again. "Competing" is defined because Layth flagged it as unclear to a lay reader.

### B. The waiting list

6. PLACES GO IN WAITING-LIST ORDER. There are 10 places (10 is Asif's example number; CONFIRM WITH ASIF the final count). Places go to complete applications by the date and time we receive them, first come, first served. Nothing else decides the order.
   WHY: Fair to everyone and easy to explain. It also means we never have to justify a judgment about who "deserved" a place.

7. AN APPLICATION IS COMPLETE ONLY WHEN THE FORM IS FILLED IN AND YOUR CONTACT DETAILS WORK. Within [2] working days [CONFIRM WITH ASIF] we send you the confirmation email. It says one of two things: that you hold a place, or your position on the waiting list. When you hold a place, the confirmation email (or a follow-up to the same thread) also gives the date, time and form of your call.
   WHY: Half-filled forms cannot hold a place ahead of people who did it properly. The confirmation email is the client's written proof of where they stand, and it is the real document clause 37 now names.

8. A PLACE BELONGS TO YOUR COMPANY AND CANNOT BE PASSED ON. It cannot be sold, swapped or handed to another business. It cannot be swapped for money, a discount or any paid alternative.
   WHY: Stops a market in free places; stops anyone asking for money or a discount in place of the audit.

9. ONCE ALL 10 PLACES ARE HELD, NEW APPLICANTS JOIN THE WAITING LIST, IN ORDER. If a place frees up (someone withdraws, misses the call under clause 18 or 19, or loses the place under clause 34), it goes to the first person on the waiting list, who is told by email. Being on the waiting list does not promise a place.
   WHY: Clear for the client about what being on the list means; protects us from a claim that a list position was a booking. "Join" replaces "stay", because someone applying late was never on the list to stay on it.

10. THE OFFER IS FOR A LIMITED TIME, WITH NO FIXED END DATE. It ends at the first of two moments: when the tenth call has taken place, or when we end the offer under clause 36. A place counts as used once its call has taken place; a place freed before its call goes back to the waiting list under clause 9. When the offer ends, the waiting list closes. Everyone still on it is told by email within [5] working days [CONFIRM WITH ASIF], their position ends, and it does not carry over to any later offer unless we say so in writing. People who already hold a place keep it (clause 36).
    WHY: Asif ruled there is no fixed end date, so the limit is the 10 places themselves. Defining "used" as "the call has happened" settles the question Layth raised: the list survives exactly as long as a freed place could still be handed on, and closes the moment none can. Telling people still waiting is courtesy and stops anyone believing they are still in line for something that has finished.

### C. What you get, and what you do not

11. WHAT THE AUDIT INCLUDES: one call of up to [60] minutes [CONFIRM WITH ASIF], held by [video / phone / in person, CONFIRM WITH ASIF], and one written read-out, sent by email within 10 working days after the call.
    WHY: A fixed, written scope is what protects both sides. The client knows what to expect; we know where the free work stops.

12. HOW WORKING DAYS ARE COUNTED. Working days are Sunday to Thursday, not counting public holidays in Bangladesh [CONFIRM WITH ASIF: Bangladesh or UK calendar]. If something outside our control delays the read-out, we tell you and give a new date.
    WHY: "10 working days" means different weeks in Dhaka and London; naming the calendar removes an argument before it starts.

13. WHAT THE AUDIT DOES NOT INCLUDE: we do not carry out any of the work the read-out suggests. There is no design, writing, building, strategy work, extra calls, unlimited follow-up questions, or rewriting of the read-out. You may send [one] email of questions about the read-out within [14] days of receiving it, and we answer it once [CONFIRM WITH ASIF].
    WHY: This is the line between a free read and free consulting. Without it, the audit grows one "quick question" at a time and nobody ever wrote that down.

14. THE READ-OUT IS A FINISHED DOCUMENT, NOT A DRAFT. We do not rewrite it to match a view you prefer. If we got a fact wrong because of a mistake on our side, tell us and we correct that fact.
    WHY: Protects the honesty of the read-out (it is a read, not an agreement); still protects the client from a plain factual error.

15. IF YOU LATER WANT PAID WORK, IT IS A NEW AND SEPARATE AGREEMENT. Nothing in the read-out is a price, a quote or a promise of what paid work would include. Paid work has its own written scope and terms.
    WHY: Stops the read-out being treated later as an agreed scope ("your audit said you would fix X"); protects the client from a hidden commitment.

### D. What we need from you

16. WE NEED HONEST, COMPLETE INFORMATION, AND THE READ-OUT RESTS ON WHAT YOU SHARE. We may send a short set of questions before the call. If the information is incomplete or wrong, the read-out will be too, and we are not responsible for that.
    WHY: Fixes where the quality of the read-out comes from; protects us from blame for gaps we could not see.

17. FALSE DETAILS BREAK THE TERMS. This includes a false company name, a false role, or hiding that you work for a competing studio. If we find false details, clause 34 applies: we may take the application off the waiting list, or pause or end the audit, and we tell you why. An honest mistake that you correct when we ask is not a false detail.
    WHY: Protects the list and the method from people who apply under a cover story. The consequence sits in clause 34 so all breaches follow one model. The honest-mistake line mirrors clause 14, where we correct our own factual errors: the same fairness runs both ways.

### E. Missed or late calls

18. IF YOU NEED TO MOVE THE CALL, TELL US AT LEAST [24] HOURS BEFORE [CONFIRM WITH ASIF]. You can move it [once]. After that, the place goes to the next person on the list.
    WHY: One rescheduling is courtesy; repeated moves hold a place others are waiting for.

19. IF YOU DO NOT JOIN THE CALL AND DID NOT TELL US, WE WAIT [15] MINUTES, THEN THE CALL IS CLOSED. The place passes to the next person on the list.
    WHY: Senior time is the whole cost of a free audit; a no-show should not cost a waiting business its turn.

20. IF YOU JOIN LATE, THE CALL STILL ENDS AT ITS BOOKED TIME.
    WHY: Keeps the call a fixed size without anyone having to argue about it in the moment.

21. IF WE NEED TO MOVE THE CALL, WE TELL YOU AS EARLY AS WE CAN AND OFFER A NEW TIME. You keep your place.
    WHY: The same rule runs both ways; the client is never penalised for our change.

### F. Who owns what

22. YOU KEEP THE READ-OUT FOR YOUR OWN BUSINESS USE. You may share it inside your company and with your own advisers (for example your accountant or lawyer).
    WHY: The client gets real use of what they received, with no fear of breaking a rule by showing it to their own team.

23. OUR METHODS STAY OURS. The questions we ask, how we structure the read-out, our templates, scoring and working tools remain the property of FlowState Impact (the name as Asif gave it; this clause uses the plain spelling, matching the definition of "we"). Receiving a read-out does not give you any right to them.
    WHY: The method is what we sell in paid work; giving away its use with every free read would empty the studio.

24. YOU MAY NOT RESELL, PUBLISH OR PASS OFF THE READ-OUT. It cannot be sold, posted publicly, used to train software, presented as your own work, or used to deliver the same service to others.
    WHY: Stops the read-out becoming someone else's product or someone's AI training material.

25. QUOTING US IN PUBLIC NEEDS OUR WRITTEN YES. Using our name, the read-out or quotes from it in marketing or the press needs our permission in writing first.
    WHY: Protects our name from being attached to claims we did not make or to work we did not do.

26. NO RECORDING OR SHARING OF THE CALL UNLESS BOTH SIDES AGREE FIRST. This includes AI note-taking tools and meeting bots that join the call. We take our own written notes.
    WHY: Protects what the client says in private; protects our working method from being captured and passed around. Naming meeting bots closes the most common modern leak.

### G. Advice, responsibility and obligation

27. THIS IS NOT LEGAL, FINANCIAL, TAX OR ACCOUNTING ADVICE. For those, speak to a qualified professional.
    WHY: Keeps us out of regulated advice we are not licensed to give; tells the client where to go for it.

28. YOUR DECISIONS ARE YOURS. The read-out is our honest view on the day it is written. We do not promise any result from following it, and we are not responsible for decisions you make or losses that follow from it, as far as the law allows. Nothing here removes rights that the law says cannot be removed.
    WHY: A free service cannot carry open-ended risk. The last sentence matters: a clause that tries to exclude everything can be thrown out entirely, so this one only excludes what can lawfully be excluded.

29. NO OBLIGATION EITHER WAY. You owe us nothing for the audit and are under no obligation to buy anything. We are under no obligation to offer you paid work. We may tell you once what paid work would look like; we will not chase you, and nothing renews or charges automatically.
    WHY: Protects the client from a hidden sale; protects us from a claim that the audit promised a working relationship.

### H. Privacy and confidentiality

30. WHAT YOU SHARE IS SEEN ONLY BY THE PEOPLE WHO PREPARE YOUR AUDIT. We use it only to prepare your audit. The people who see it are our own team. We pass it to nobody else, with one exception: when the law requires us to, and then we tell you, unless the law forbids telling. The services that store our email and files are named in the privacy notice (clause 32).
    FALLBACK, used only if Asif confirms a freelancer ever works on audits: add "and any freelancer we hire to help on your audit, who has agreed in writing to keep it private and to use it only for your audit". Without his yes, this line does not appear.
    [CONFIRM WITH ASIF: does anyone outside the team ever see audit material, including a freelancer, or software that reads it, such as AI tools used to prepare the read-out? If AI tools read client material, this clause and the privacy notice must say so plainly. Ilyas to read this clause either way.]
    WHY: The old heading promised the details never leave the studio, then the body let a contractor see them; one of the two had to give. The heading now promises only what the body keeps. "Trusted" is gone: it praised ourselves and gave the reader nothing to hold us to. The exception for a freelancer, if it ever exists, is defined by what that person has signed, not by our opinion of them.

31. WE ASK BEFORE USING ANYTHING, EVEN WITHOUT YOUR NAME. If we want to use a lesson from your audit with your name and details removed (for example in an article), we ask you first in writing. Using your name or company in any case study needs a separate written yes.
    WHY: Asking rather than assuming builds trust and avoids any argument about whether a detail could identify them.

32. YOUR PERSONAL DETAILS ARE HANDLED UNDER OUR PRIVACY NOTICE [CONFIRM WITH ASIF: no privacy page exists yet]. We keep audit material for [12] months, then delete it, unless you become a paid client [CONFIRM WITH ASIF].
    WHY: UK data protection law applies to any UK client whatever law governs these terms. A retention limit protects them and us.

33. WE DO NOT ADD YOU TO A MAILING LIST UNLESS YOU SAY YES.
    WHY: Respect, and it keeps us inside email marketing rules in both countries.

### I. When a rule is broken

34. WHEN ONE OF FIVE RULES IS BROKEN, WE MAY TAKE AN APPLICATION OFF THE WAITING LIST, OR PAUSE OR END AN AUDIT. This is the only clause that sets out what happens after a breach; clauses 5, 17, 24 and 26 point here. The five rules: (1) false details, clause 17; (2) working for or applying for a competing studio, clause 5; (3) recording or sharing the call without agreement, clause 26; (4) reselling, publishing or misusing the read-out, clause 24; (5) rude, threatening or abusive behaviour towards anyone in the studio. We use this power for these five reasons only, never for any other. We tell you in writing which rule was broken. A pause is used while we check something; during a pause you keep your place, and we tell you when we will decide. If an audit ends before its call, the place goes to the first person on the waiting list (clause 9).
    WHY, ONE MODEL FOR EVERY BREACH, AND WHY THIS ONE: the draft said a breach "ends" the audit in clauses 5 and 17 but "may" end it in clause 34. I chose "may" for all five, on the merits. Automatic ending sounds fairer but is not: it would end an audit over a typo in a job title, and "rude behaviour" cannot be automatic anyway, because someone has to judge it. "May" lets the response fit the breach (a pause to check a fact, an ending for a cover story). The risk of "may" is that it reads as the studio choosing who gets a place, which Asif has ruled out; that risk is closed by three fences: the five reasons are a closed list, the clause says no other reason is used, and every use is explained in writing. "Decline" is dropped from the heading and the body because it hints at selection; "take an application off the waiting list" says the same thing tied to a broken rule.
    WORDING NOTE FOR EHSAN: name the rules by their headings as well as their numbers, so the cross-references survive any renumbering (Layth advisory E).

35. WE STEP BACK WHERE THERE IS A CONFLICT OF INTEREST. A conflict of interest means that running your audit would mean using what another client told us in private. If that happens, we tell you that we cannot run your audit while the conflict lasts. You keep your position on the waiting list, and if the conflict ends while the offer is still open, your audit goes ahead in its turn.
    WHY: Protects every client's confidences, including this one's later on. The term is now defined in the clause itself, so "the conflict ends" has something to refer to (Layth must-fix 6, at fact level only).

### J. General

36. WE MAY CHANGE OR END THE OFFER. Changes are posted on this page with the date. If we end the offer, the waiting list closes as clause 10 describes. A change or an ending never takes away a place already held, or the call and read-out already promised to someone holding one.
    WHY: Lets us adjust a live offer; protects people already holding a place from moving goalposts. The ending now points to clause 10, so both routes to an ending treat the waiting list the same way.

37. THESE TERMS AND YOUR CONFIRMATION EMAIL (CLAUSE 7) ARE THE WHOLE AGREEMENT FOR THE AUDIT. "Whole agreement" means nothing said in a chat, a call or another message adds to them. If one clause turns out not to be valid, the rest still stand.
    WHY: Stops side promises in a chat or call being read as extra terms; stops one weak clause sinking the page. The draft named a "booking confirmation" that exists nowhere; the real document is the confirmation email of clause 7, which carries the client's place and call time, so it is named instead of removed.

38. GOVERNING LAW: THE LAW OF BANGLADESH, AND THE COURTS OF DHAKA [ASIF TO CONFIRM]. If a problem arises, both sides first try to settle it by talking, in writing, for [30] days. Only if that fails does it go to the courts of Dhaka. This does not take away any right that the law of your own country gives you and does not allow to be taken away.
    WHY: The studio is registered in Dhaka, so the law sits where the party making these promises can actually stand in court, and a dispute is nearer and cheaper for the side giving free work. The last sentence keeps the clause honest with a London client: some protections (UK data protection among them, clause 32) apply whatever law is named, and saying so stops the clause overreaching. Talking first keeps a small free offer from becoming a lawsuit.

39. CONTACT. Questions about these terms go to [EMAIL, CONFIRM WITH ASIF: no contact email exists on the site yet; this blocks going live]. Company: FlowState Impact, registered in Dhaka, Bangladesh. Registration number and registered address: [CONFIRM WITH ASIF, if any exist; if none, the line shows the name and "Dhaka, Bangladesh" only]. Last updated: [the date the page is published].
    WHY: A terms page with no named party and no contact is weak if anyone ever tests it. The page states no company type (Asif's ruling).

## 3. QUESTIONS ONLY ASIF CAN ANSWER

1. GOVERNING LAW: CONFIRM BANGLADESH LAW AND DHAKA COURTS (now drafted into clause 38).
   - For: the business is registered in Dhaka, the team and the work sit there, so a dispute is nearer and cheaper; the law sits where the promising party can stand in court.
   - Against: London clients find it unfamiliar, and contract outcomes there are less predictable. England and Wales would read better to them, but defending a claim in London from Dhaka costs real money and travel, and reads oddly for a business not registered in the UK.
   - RECOMMEND: Bangladesh law, Dhaka courts, as drafted. Flip if the business registers in the UK, or most applicants turn out to be in the UK.
2. HOW MANY PLACES: SETTLED IN PART. Asif ruled no fixed end date; the offer ends when the tenth call has taken place (clause 10). Still open: confirm 10 as the final count (it was his example), and the [5] working days to tell the waiting list when the offer ends.
3. REGISTRATION NUMBER AND REGISTERED ADDRESS: the name is settled (FlowState Impact, registered in Dhaka). Confirm whether a registration number and a registered address exist and should show. A fact only you hold.
4. HOW LONG IS THE CALL, AND IS IT VIDEO, PHONE OR IN PERSON? Recommend 60 minutes by video. It keeps Dhaka and London clients on equal footing.
5. ONE EMAIL OF FOLLOW-UP QUESTIONS, OR NONE? Recommend one email within 14 days, answered once. Zero feels cold after a free read; one is generous and still has an edge.
6. WHICH CALENDAR DO WORKING DAYS USE? Recommend Bangladesh working days, stated plainly, because that is where the read-out is written.
7. RESCHEDULE AND NO-SHOW RULES: recommend one move with 24 hours notice, and a 15 minute wait before a no-show passes the place on.
8. PRIVACY NOTICE AND RETENTION: there is no privacy page yet. Recommend writing one before this page goes live, with a 12 month keep-then-delete rule for audit material. It must name the services that store client material, which clause 30 now relies on.
9. WHO SEES AUDIT MATERIAL: does anyone outside the team (a freelancer, or AI tools used to prepare the read-out) ever see what a client shares? Clause 30 is written for "our own team only" until you say otherwise. Recommend: if AI tools read client material, say so in clause 30 and the privacy notice; a client who later finds it unsaid would read the whole page as untrue.
